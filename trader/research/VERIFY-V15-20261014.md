## Judge: **modify_v15** (conf 0.72)

All six reviewers agree on the direction: no one argues to revert, and every 'weakens' verdict attacks the size of the claimed edge and the specific clauses, not the switch itself. I re-checked the disputed points against the data files; scripts are in /tmp/claude-0/-home-user-test-trading/0c00e7d9-c916-50ae-ac83-8f6429819f25/scratchpad/wf/judge/.

(1) The BASKET-20261013 headline comparison is wrong. With each basket in cash on its own earnings nights, as both rulebooks require, over 257 nights NVDL makes +0.384%/night (sd 2.62, score 0.147) and old METU2+NVDL1 makes +0.253% (sd 2.17, score 0.116). The difference is +0.131%/night with t=1.01. Over the last 137 nights the old basket is ahead (-0.053%, t=-0.26). Over the last 60 they are tied (+0.098%, t=0.34). 'Worst in every window' and '+$0.40 vs +$0.18' do not hold.

(2) Picking NVDA is hindsight. No ex-ante long-window rule picks it, and NVDL's lead over an equal-weight universe is t of about 1.1. However, the rule v1.5 replaced, the 30-night rank, has no predictive power (monthly IC about 0). A realistic whole-share version of old §3 scores about 0.08 at about 70% invested, against about 0.14-0.15 for fixed NVDA-2x out of sample. NVDA-2x is also positive over the long run: synthetic 2x NVDA ex-earnings over 7.8 years averages +0.22%/night, though 2022 was -45bp/night. So a fixed NVDA-2x core is a reasonable, simple, low-churn choice. It is not a proven better one, and the planning edge should be about +0.30%/night gross per unit of exposure, not +0.46%.

(3) Rule (b) is broken. On the agent's own 10/9 file, MUU's 250-night score was 0.1598 against NVDL's 0.1572, so MUU qualified. The comparison sits inside one standard error (about 0.063), and walk-forward tests show satellites add nothing.

(4) The m_NVDL vs m_NVDX split in (c) is a data artifact. Adding back NVDX's 2025-12-24 distribution and fixing the 2026-09-08 bad open gives NVDX +0.448% vs NVDL +0.458%, a difference of 1.0bp (t=0.97). NVDX also costs about 3-6bp more per night to trade.

(5) Sizing. 97% is below full Kelly even on the 7.8-year data (f* 1.80; 1.15 with the mean cut by one standard error), so heavy exposure is defensible for a growth goal. NVDX x5 is still effectively dominated: NVDL2+NVDX1 has about the same EV per night after costs with about 9% less exposure. Filling NVDL first naturally lands at about 79-98% invested, about 89% tonight.

(6) The largest risk is a bear regime, not a single night: 7.8-year maxDD is -81% at 97%. A trailing-120-night edge-decay switch, checked on Mondays, cut that to -61% and cut 2022 from -73% to -24% with no loss of growth. It would never have fired in the past 257 nights. That makes it the cheapest robust protection, and it matches the statistics lens's pre-registered review trigger.

### Critic (would change decision: False)

Completeness check of the six lens reports and the judge's modify_v15 decision. None of the gaps changes the top-level decision. No lens shows the old rule is better ex-ante: v1.5's own procedure scores 0.064-0.074 against 0.075-0.085 for the old rule, so procedure against procedure they tie. NVDA-2x has lower variance and churn, and the corrected comparison is a wash rather than a loss. Three findings should change the judge's write-up and sizing rationale.

(1) The day leg was never modelled. The TZA day leg runs at about 90% of E on 3 days in 5, and no lens or the judge modelled it alongside the overnight leg. Bootstrapping both legs together, P(PAUSE in 60 nights) at tonight's 88.6% goes from 4.9% to 20.5% on 1-year data and from 35.9% to 47.0% on the 7.8-year synthetic. Over 125 nights on 1-year data it goes from 9% to 40%. Over a full year the day leg (TP rule simulated on TZA daily bars) shows about zero mean (-0.026%/day, skew -1.89). The judge argued against trimming the overnight core because growth is needed. If the human wants less drawdown, the day leg is the cheaper cut, and the PAUSE odds the judge quoted are understated by roughly 3x on 1-year data.

(2) The §3(f) edge-decay switch is not free. The judge's numbers reproduce exactly. The drawdown cut holds up across every weekday phase, window and threshold I tried (-35% to -66% vs -81%). Growth parity, however, comes entirely from 2022: excluding 2022, the switch cuts log growth about 23% (0.253% to 0.194%/night). It turned 2025 from +9% into -33% and 2020 from +100% into +39%. It nearly fires on the evidence window: NVDL ETF trailing-120 minimum is +0.052%, and the synthetic series fired in September 2025. It should be adopted, if at all, as stated insurance with a known cost.

(3) The judge's §6 text is factually wrong. There were 4 NVDA non-earnings gaps of -10% or worse in 7.8 years, not 16, and none of -15% or worse; the 16 is the count of gaps of -5.2% or worse.

Smaller points:
- The judge's main pro-switch argument compares a hindsight fixed pick against an ex-ante rule. That rests on one lens's approximate 'realistic old §3' sim, which nobody cross-checked.
- 'NVDX x5 dominated' is really a tie.
- Untested: macro event nights inside the hold (CPI/NFP at 08:30; the 2026-11-03 midterm; the 2022 midterm night was -6% at 2x), the 100%-NVDA PAUSE fallback, half-day edge, ETF/issuer risk, and the anomaly's mechanism.
- Confirmed: the judge's overnight-only PAUSE bootstrap numbers (within Monte Carlo noise) and the NVDA 2026-11-17 pm earnings date (Robinhood, verified=true).

**Critic gaps:**
- The biggest gap is that nobody modelled the TZA day leg and the overnight leg together. The day leg is active: 12 active days, running 3 days in every 5 at about 90% of E. Every PAUSE and KILL figure behind the judge's sizing decision covers the overnight leg only. I bootstrapped both legs together (60-night horizon, block 5, E0 104.6, rolling-20 high, TP +1% simulated from the daily high, 3-on/2-off schedule). P(PAUSE) at 88.6% overnight goes from 4.9% to 20.5% on 1-year data and from 35.9% to 47.0% on the 7.8-year synthetic. At 97% it goes from 7.4% to 24.5% and from 41.1% to 52.3%. Over 125 nights at 88.6% on 1-year data it goes from 9.0% to 39.9%. A Gaussian iid check agrees: 5.1% to 14.8%. Over a full year the day leg shows no edge. With the TP rule applied to TZA daily bars it averages -0.026%/day (sd 1.92%, skew -1.89, TP hit on 71% of days, 5th percentile -4.5%). The 7.8-year synthetic -3x IWM version averages +0.017%/day. So the day leg adds most of the short-horizon PAUSE risk while showing no long-sample edge. The judge's argument that cutting overnight exposure costs growth for little PAUSE reduction ignores it; if risk has to come down, the day leg is the cheaper cut. Caveat: live day legs net about +$3.25 over 12 legs (state.json), and open/close are proxies for the 09:32 entry and 15:49 exit.
- §3(f) edge-decay switch: the judge's 'no loss of growth / no in-sample cost' claim is overstated. I reproduced the judge's numbers exactly (W120 Monday: log growth 0.155% vs 0.153%, maxDD -60.8% vs -81.0%, 2022 -24% vs -73%). But all of the growth parity comes from 2022. Excluding 2022, the switch cuts mean log growth from 0.253% to 0.194%/night, about 23% lower. It cost money in the V-shaped recoveries: 2025 +9% becomes -33% (OFF 2025-04-07 to 07-28), 2020 +100% becomes +39% (OFF 2020-03-09 to 06-29), 2019 +45% becomes +17%. Growth moves from 0.153 to 0.174 depending on which weekday the re-rank falls, and from 0.134 to 0.203 across W80-200 and thresholds of ±0.1%. The maxDD cut is robust (-35% to -66% vs -81% in every variant). The switch sits close to firing: on the synthetic series it went OFF 2025-09-08 to 09-22, just before the evidence window. The NVDL ETF trailing-120 minimum is +0.052% (window ending 2026-04-28), and 9% of windows sit below +0.10%. It should be presented as insurance that pays in slow bears and costs in fast rebounds, not as free.
- The judge's justification for keeping NVDA compares the wrong things. It sets a hindsight-picked fixed NVDA-2x (score 0.14-0.15) against an ex-ante old rule (0.075-0.085). An ex-ante version of v1.5's own procedure scored 0.064-0.074, so procedure against procedure the switch is no better. Only one lens (selection-bias) computed the 'realistic old §3' number. It approximated sizing (no v1.2 partial unit, fixed E, 68% invested even though v1.1 targets at least 70%) and nobody cross-checked it. Invested fraction does not change the mean/sd score, so the 'at about 70% invested' framing does not explain the gap. The defensible case is lower variance and less churn, not a demonstrated edge.
- The judge's proposed §6 text has a factual error. '-10..-15% NVDA non-earnings gap (16 such nights in 7.8 years)' is wrong. The 16 nights are NVDA gaps of -5.2% or worse, which cost at least 10% of E at 97% x2. NVDA non-earnings gaps of -10% or worse: 4 (2019-01-28 -14.8%, 2024-08-05 -14.2%, 2025-01-27 -12.5%, 2020-03-16 -11.8%). Gaps of -15% or worse: 0. At 88.6% exposure, an instant PAUSE from at-high needs an NVDA gap of about -11.3%; 4 such nights in 7.8 years.
- Untested: scheduled macro events that fall inside the hold. 08:30 CPI/NFP releases land before the 09:31 exit, and the 2026-11-03 midterm night is ahead. The 2022-11-08 midterm night was -6.0% for 2x NVDA (2020: +5.7%, 2024: +4.4%). §3's 'edge survives macro nights' rests on older research nobody re-checked.
- Untested: the alternative PAUSE fallback. 1 NVDL share is still 100% NVDA. The risk lens flagged this and the judge kept it without testing cash or a lower fraction.
- Also untested: whether the overnight edge holds after a 13:00 close (the judge's 12:52 half-day entry); swap-based ETF and issuer risk, including NAV premium at the open; the mechanism or crowding behind NVDA's overnight anomaly, which matters for persistence; and single-point operational risk (a missed or late exit with 89-97% in one underlying).

- §3(f) is described as insurance with a known growth cost, not as free.

- The critic's day-leg finding is open: the day leg has no full-year edge and roughly triples short-horizon PAUSE risk. A separate day-leg review workflow ran the same day: `DAYLEG-REVIEW-20261014.md`.

## Open questions
- Does the human prefer a lower drawdown over growth? Fixed ~69-75% invested cuts P(PAUSE in 60 nights) from 4.5% to 0.8% on the 1y data (36% to 24% on the 7.8y data) but costs about 20-27% of log growth, and the $200 goal is already only ~16-18% likely within 125 nights.
- Should a volatility brake be added? Trailing-60 ex-earnings sd > 3.5% -> 1 NVDL share helped on the 7.8y data at 3.5-4.0% thresholds but not at 4.5%. It is threshold-sensitive and was not adopted.
- Edge-decay window: weekly W60 looked better than W120 on the 7.8y data (maxDD -45% vs -61%) but would have switched to cash for 5 nights in May 2026. W120 was chosen because it never fired in-sample. Either is defensible, but pre-register one and do not tune it later.
- The long-run evidence is synthetic (2x NVDA close->open minus 4bp drag), and NVDA earnings dates before 2025 came from the risk lens's memory list. NVDA is also the decade's hindsight winner, so the long-run mean (+0.22%/night ex-earnings) is still optimistic.
- An equal-weight universe matched NVDL out of sample (0.160 vs 0.152 from April) with no selection risk, but it cannot be held with whole shares at E=$105. Revisit a 2-3 name diversified core if E grows past about $250.
- The real NVDX bid/ask at 09:31 is unmeasured (no historical quotes). If live NVDX exit slippage proves worse than about 10bp, swap the NVDX filler for cash rather than chase the extra ~11% of exposure.

# Strategy review, 2026-11-09: how to get better returns

Asked by the human on Mon 11/9 after the close: "inspect our plan and see how we can get better returns, then make the actual changes, and keep recursively self improving."

## Result in one paragraph

One change survived: cut the overnight position to a quarter while QQQ is below its 200-day average (RULES_v3 §13). On 26 years of data it raised compound growth and roughly halved the worst drawdown of a 2x NVDA overnight position. An independent re-check reproduced every number and found no look-ahead, but it rates the size of the gain as unproven: ten nights supply nearly all of it. Read the rule as insurance against a long bear market, bought at little or no cost in growth, and not as extra return. Seven other ideas were tested and rejected. Two small execution changes were made alongside it: one symbol instead of two, and a fractional top-up so that "fully invested" really is 98% rather than whatever whole shares allow. A weekly research loop (§14, `trader/LAB.md`) now repeats this process.

The honest scale: at E ≈ $88 the engine's expected gain is about 25–35 cents a night. No rule change at this size produces big dollar gains. The levers that would are capital (only the human adds it), leverage (options, waiting on the human's application) and variance.

## Data and method

- Day bars from the broker, 2000-01-03 → 2026-11-06, split-adjusted: NVDA, QQQ, SPY, SMH, and ten more large technology names (AMD, INTC, MU, AAPL, MSFT, AMZN, QCOM, TXN, AMAT, ORCL). From 2015 also TQQQ, SOXL, TECL, UPRO; NVDL from 12/2022 and NVDX from 10/2023.
- ON = close → next open. ID = open → close. Synthetic leveraged series are k × the underlying's return for that leg, less 9bp per night for costs. They are not the real ETFs.
- g = mean log growth per night in percent. This is the number that compounds; it is lower than the mean when volatility is high.
- Every state used for sizing is known at the close before the night it sizes.

## What changed (RULES_v3 v2.1, engine v1.8, from the night-45 entry on 11/10)

2. **§3(c) one symbol plus a fractional top-up.** Whole NVDL shares by limit order, then one dollar-based market order for the rest of the target if it is at least $2. This replaces the NVDX filler. Tool: `tools/entry_v18.py`; exit of the fraction: `tools/exit_frac.py`.

## Caveats

- The filter is one survivor of roughly 30 variants. Expect its real benefit to be smaller than the table.
- Out of sample it works through lower variance and smaller drawdowns, not through a lower mean return below the line. It is risk control more than prediction.
- It loses in V-shaped recoveries: a quarter position on the way back up. Expected cost when wrong: about 0.02–0.09 percentage points a night.
- T8 does not remove NVDA earnings nights (the live engine sits those out).
- Synthetic 2× ignores the real ETFs' fees, financing and tracking error beyond the flat 9bp.
- On 11/9 QQQ is 0.35% from the line. At that distance the state is close to a coin flip.

## Rollback

Not on live P&L alone (40 DOWN nights cannot separate ±0.3% a night). At each quarterly review, first on 2027-01-11, the long-history test is re-run with new data. Revert to always-full if an error is found in this evidence, if the updated test no longer shows the rule ahead for NVDA-2x and for at least 8 of the 11 names, or if the human says so.

## Independent verification (finished 17:20 ET 11/9, before the first v1.8 entry)

A separate read-only agent rebuilt the test from the raw bar files with its own loader and code. It did not read the scripts behind the tables above.

**Verdict: weakly supported.** The numbers reproduce and the direction is robust. The size of the gain is not pinned down. Rollback criterion (i) is not triggered, so §13 stays in force from night 45. Read the rule as insurance against a long bear market that has cost little or nothing in growth, not as an edge worth 0.06% a night.

What it confirmed:

- **Reproduction.** Every figure in both T8 tables matches to three decimals, as do the 9 / 11 / 10 of 11 counts.
- **No look-ahead.** Removing the data after the close never changed the state (600 random checks). Lagging the state by one day moves 2000–26 growth from +0.163 to +0.165. A deliberate one-day look-ahead would have added about 0.04.
- **Sensitivity.** 72 cells (QQQ or SPY; 100, 150, 200 or 250 days; defensive size 0, 0.25 or 0.5; three periods) are all positive. QQQ with 200 days sits next to the best cell. SPY is weaker everywhere. The weakest cells are 100 days in 2015–26 (+0.008 to +0.015).
- **Placebo.** With the QQQ state shifted in time at random, the mean gain was −0.017 (5–95% range −0.048 to +0.015). None of 3,000 draws reached the observed +0.060.
- **Timing, not just less exposure.** A constant position with the same average exposure (0.816) grew +0.097 a night against the rule's +0.163. Across the 11 names the rule beats that benchmark for 10, with a median edge of +0.026.
- **Bootstrap, rule minus always-full, 2000–26:** +0.060 a night; 90% interval with year blocks +0.009 to +0.121; the rule was ahead in 98% of draws.

What weakens it:

- **Concentration.** Ten nights supply about 98% of the NVDA gain. Three of them supply 55% (2004-08-05, 2008-07-02, 2002-07-30, all company-specific gaps of −27% to −31% at 2×). The years 2008 and 2022 supply 73%.
- **Most of the time it loses a little.** It lost in 51 of 82 spells below the line (median length 4 nights), in 9 of 22 years that had DOWN nights, and in 2010–14 as a whole (−0.026). Spells of 20 nights or fewer cost 1.03 log points in total; the longer ones made 4.87. The two kinds cannot be told apart in advance: the first five nights of all spells together still favoured the rule by 0.55.
- **Neither half is significant alone.** Year-block 90% intervals: 2000–14 −0.013 to +0.162; 2015–26 −0.002 to +0.128.
- **Earnings nights.** Without the 47 earnings nights (the live engine sits them out) the 2015–26 gain is +0.046. Without two pre-announcement nights as well it is +0.032, with an interval that includes zero.
- **Real funds.** NVDL has 86 DOWN nights in 6 spells: rule minus full +0.020 a night (interval −0.029 to +0.090, t 0.55), drawdown −58% → −37%. NVDX has 59: +0.022. Same sign, no statistical weight. NVDL ran at about 1.5× until early 2024. Since April 2024 the real funds trail the synthetic 2× overnight by 0.03% (NVDL) and 0.07% (NVDX) a night.
- **The cost model flatters the rule.** The flat 9bp scales with size, which is worth 0.016 of the 0.060. At zero cost the gain is +0.044.
- **"10 of 11 names" is closer to one test than eleven.** Always-full has negative growth for 7 of the 11 names, so any cut helps there, and the names share the same DOWN nights. "Either below" is the best rule for 7 names; the QQQ rule is best for NVDA, MU and MSFT.
- **Growth rewards cutting an over-levered position whatever the signal.** The best constant fraction of 2× NVDA was 0.65 in 2000–14 and 1.70 in 2015–26.

Where the gain comes from, per DOWN night: before 2015 the mean return on DOWN nights was positive, and the whole benefit is lower variance drag. Since 2015 a negative mean on DOWN nights adds to it. DOWN nights have about 1.8 times the standard deviation of UP nights (5.6% against 3.2%).

Data problems found:

- The broker's NVDA bars up to 2000-10-27 are priced 10× too high (a missing split adjustment). Both implementations drop that stretch. With the bars repaired instead, 2000–26 reads +0.079 for always-full and +0.154 for the rule (interval +0.019 to +0.137), so the error hid a period in which the rule helped.
- Prices are not dividend-adjusted. This is negligible for NVDA and biases always-full down for the dividend payers.

A by-product that needs its own test: the 47 NVDA earnings nights supplied about 44% of always-full's growth in 2015–26 (+0.184 a night with them, +0.104 without). The engine's earnings blackout (§3(d)) gives that up. It is now lab item L12. It does not change the plan for 11/17.
